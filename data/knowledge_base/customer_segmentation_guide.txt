CUSTOMER SEGMENTATION GUIDE

Purpose:
Customer segmentation is used to group customers according to their purchasing behavior and value.

RFM Analysis:

RFM stands for:

Recency:
How recently a customer interacted or purchased.

Frequency:
How frequently a customer makes purchases.

Monetary:
The monetary value associated with customer purchases.

Business Interpretation:

High-value customers generally have stronger purchasing activity and higher monetary value.

Standard-value customers generally have lower purchasing frequency and monetary value compared with high-value customers.

Customer Segments:

HIGH VALUE CUSTOMERS:
These customers demonstrate relatively high purchase frequency and monetary value.

Recommended Actions:
- Prioritize customer retention.
- Provide personalized recommendations.
- Monitor churn risk.
- Consider loyalty programs.
- Provide targeted offers where appropriate.

STANDARD VALUE CUSTOMERS:
These customers have comparatively lower purchase frequency and monetary value.

Recommended Actions:
- Encourage repeat purchases.
- Provide product recommendations.
- Monitor engagement.
- Use targeted campaigns to increase customer value.

Important RFM Note:

RFM analysis should ideally use actual transaction dates to calculate recency. If transaction dates are unavailable, a proxy measure may be used, but the limitation should be documented.

Segmentation Limitation:

Customer segmentation describes groups of customers based on behavioral characteristics. A segment should not automatically be interpreted as a churn prediction. Churn prediction and customer segmentation serve different analytical purposes.
